# Materials and light in the packed rooms

The [browser review](../public/lighting-review/index.html) (`/lighting-review/` when served) uses frames from the running website. Its earlier hero images are from `383154b` with the same camera, exposure, sky angle and orrery phase. The separate [asset catalogue](../public/asset-catalogue/index.html) is photographed in Blender; it is not evidence of texture resolution or shading in the browser.

## What changed

- **Atrium:** shaped reception desk ends, an optical projector with nested baffles and retainers, and machined sections and supports on the orrery. The door kit has recessed louvres, a threshold and sign mounting hardware. Inspection targets and ring pivots are unchanged.
- **Observation Lounge and Library:** case-bound books have boards, paper blocks and rounded spines. The distant upright bindings use simpler geometry; they do not repeat the full close-up model thousands of times. Open atlases have curved printed leaves, a ribbon and their own 2,048 px lighting atlas. The illustration is original, non-portfolio material. Wing-chair arms/wings, cushion boxing/welts, tailored sofa throws and the shared material finishes are now lit in the rooms. Lined Lounge curtains have load-bearing runners on the pole.
- **Fabrication:** the bridge, trolley, hoist and hardware read as a load path. The suspended satellite has a built-up bus, service details, optics, panel frames, wiring and segmented cells. It is still a static exhibit; the update doesn't make the gantry or the wings move.
- **Navigation:** the table has a recessed optical assembly, rim pockets, service panels, fine chart lines and a physical azimuth scale. The stations are easier to distinguish from their reflections in the smoked glass. The dome and drum sheets face the occupants: with the previous outward normals, Cycles' two-sided camera render saw the lining but its one-sided lightmap baked the outside. Flipping the lining, closing the base moulding and rebaking fixes this without changing room exposure. A similar camera-facing check caught the unlined curtains.

## Which light is baked, which depends on the eye?

The full-size base atlases (up to 4,096 px) use **Blender 5.0.1 / Cycles, up to 192 samples, adaptive threshold 0.015, up to eight bounces**. The smaller angular passes use their own resolution and sample budgets. The Razer's RTX 4070 Laptop ran OptiX for the Atrium, Fabrication, Library and corrected Navigation. The GTX 1050 Ti ran CUDA for the final Lounge. `blender/bake.py` encodes scene-linear HDR before the site tone maps it. Window rooms retain six phases of sky and solar bounce. The Atrium also retains three phases of the orrery's light. The unchanged exterior-only Cycles backdrop can be reused; it is not an indoor lightmap.

**Fixed light and contact shadows belong to the surfaces.** Do not move them when the camera moves. The direct window sun and its shadow map, on the other hand, follow the 120-second roll. The shadow camera looks through the physical opening; a 3×3 filter compares each sampled texel with the depth of the receiving triangle at that sun ray. A single reference depth for all nine taps striped the Lounge table under grazing light. A millimetre-scale bias handles numerical error without pulling the shadow away from the caster. The drone and open book cast *and receive* that light. Their PBR materials also receive the room's sun-free, path-traced environment, not a studio light or a second copy of the live solar disc. Fixed lamp shadows and bounced light are not retraced for the flying drone.

**Reflections belong to the eye:**

1. The floor uses the current, mirrored camera, with reflection blur based on roughness and the distance to the reflected surface. Opaque drone parts write their distance too. The sky is at infinity for each rendering eye, including the mirror.
2. Polished fittings do not bake a first-hit glossy lobe into the base. A local 128-sample Cycles panorama (`blender/reflection_probe.py`) is filtered into five roughness layers in linear radiance. The site box-projects the reflected ray and uses the material's normal-incidence reflectance and roughness. Window-lit polished batches also get a shadowed directional highlight. This is **one static probe per room**, not ray-traced interreflection between every pair of moving objects. Broad sheen on rough surfaces is still baked.
3. A separate planar pass on the Navigation chart captures the *live projected models and reader* from the eye reflected across the glass, over the static probe. It does not draw the entire room again. Reader annotations aren't physical light emitters in this pass. On the draft Navigation pack at 1280×720, tier 1, the chart pass added one target and 26 calls in the hero framing; an off/on/on/off run on the HD 630 gave median times 22.2 / 23.9 / 23.2 / 22.7 ms. This is a cost, not a claimed speed-up.

## Results from the finished website

- **Five decks, two eyes each:** a reflection-only test lamp appeared within **2.1 px** of its predicted virtual image in all ten 960×540 views. On the Atrium, Lounge and Library window maps, moving the eye alone changed **0 / 65,536** sampled depths in each room. Advancing the roll changed **65,510 / 39,263 / 59,363** respectively. Fabrication and Navigation do not have a rolling window sun.
- **Navigation:** toggling reflections on and off at the same 247 world points from two eyes changed the reflected contribution by a mean of 43.6 byte levels on the chart and 25.8 on the deck (largest RGB-channel change per point). The reflection-only lamp landed within **0.84 px** of the predicted virtual image on the chart. That registration checks the moving eye, not just whether two screenshots differ.
- **Lounge:** with the light held fixed, moving the camera changed **0 of 200,704** sun-depth samples, **0 floor-visibility samples**, and no matrix entries. With the camera held still, 18 seconds of station roll changed **120,041 depth samples** and **81,771 floor-visibility samples**. Before the grazing-light correction, a flat unobstructed test surface falsely shadowed itself at **140,875** sample points. After: **0**. A touching block still cast a dark contact shadow and the up-light side stayed lit.
- **Library:** with the roll held fixed, a real fetch moved the drone's shadow (2,121 changed caster-mask samples). Removing only direct sun from its materials affected 3,016 pixels at the cradle and 3,073 in flight. Opaque surface distances in its floor reflection agreed with 23 mesh ray hits to within **1 mm**. A separate browser visit requested 02, replaced it with 04, verified both pages opened on the requested volume each time, then pressed Escape: no book remained on the tray and the drone returned to within 6 mm of its measured cradle position.
- **Browser and package:** Chromium and headless WebKit at 960×540 drew measurable opaque baked surfaces in every room. A second headless WebKit pass at 1280×720, with the quality governor left on its visitor settings, did too (tier 0 / scale 1 in these sampled frames). Reduced motion suppressed the default roll and the opening camera move. The Chromium tour crossed the Lounge and Library doors, rode the Ring Line and used Back/Forward without a page reload; renderer-reported geometry, texture and program counts repeated across three complete rounds. From a checkout without generated assets, the packer reproduced **all 134 files** across the five room directories byte for byte. These are local browser and packaging checks, not real Safari, WAN load-time or sustained device testing.

## Reproduce the checks

- Pack a fresh checkout before serving it: `tools/get-pack-tools.sh`, `python3 tools/pack.py`, `python3 tools/serve.py 127.0.0.1 8123`. The packer checks all required layers before replacing a working room. An extra stale layer is not encoded. A missing `probe_c3.webp` was rejected without replacing the previous destination. See the [README](../README.md) for the build, probe and bake commands, including the Windows OptiX runner.
- In the repository's Docker/Playwright environment run `node lighting.mjs URL outdir all`. The opt-in `?lightcheck=1` uses the same room update, shadow, reflection and post passes as the normal page; it can supply a deterministic eye/target/time. The test compares reflection on/off at **the same physical points**, and checks red test lamps against the geometrically predicted image in two planes. `node floor-check.mjs URL` repeats the virtual-image registration on a bare patch of deck in every room, and compares sun depths in every window room. It samples the actual sun depth and the receiver shader over a fixed world grid, first moving the eye, then rotating the sun. In the Library it isolates the actual drone from the sun map and the window term from its PBR materials during a real request. None of the temporary test lamps or layer masks are in the visitor's scene.
- `node inspect-finish.mjs URL outdir about projects writing` captures the actual pick poses, including a delivered, open volume. The additional closer Lounge detail shot is labelled. `node light-clips.mjs URL outdir /ms-playwright/ffmpeg-.../ffmpeg-linux` records the two short films from live framebuffer draws.
- `node detsnap.mjs ORIGIN OUT 10 'experience=/experience/?shot=hero&tier=0&scale=1&rollat=0&chand=0'` produces a seeded, pinned hero; substitute the other room IDs. `python3 tools/finish-gallery.py SHOTS` combines `matched-before/`, `after/`, `inspect/`, `clips/` and the matched `diagnostics/grazing-{before,after}.png` images without substituting studio stills. For the latter, `node grazing.mjs BEFORE AFTER SHOTS/diagnostics` uses the same tabletop camera in the pre-correction (`0a2de89`) and final builds.
- `node light-compat.mjs URL chromium 960 540` (or `webkit`) checks default reduced motion and compares actual fully lit frames to ones with the rough base batches disabled. A loader disappearing is not sufficient if the opaque bakes are black. Use `LIGHT_DEFAULT=1` to repeat this with the site's own adaptive quality settings. `node finish-bench.mjs BEFORE_URL AFTER_URL 150` warms up each visit before timing both packs on the same GPU.
