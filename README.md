# Adithya Rajendran — Personal Website

Public Website: [https://adithya-rajendran.com](https://adithya-rajendran.com)

## Overview

A writing-first personal website about systems, robotic vision, AI, and possible futures. The design pairs NASA's photograph of an orbital sunrise and a quiet starfield with readable field notes. Professional experience, projects, and a résumé remain one click away.

## Features and Technologies

- **Next.js**: App Router, Server Components, Cache Components and tagged Sanity caching, refreshed by the Sanity webhook and a daily cron.
- **React & TypeScript**: Robust, scalable, and type-safe development environment.
- **Tailwind CSS**: CSS-first Tailwind 4 over the Deep Field design tokens in `styles/`: two themes, Void (dark, the default) and Flight Manual (light). From 960px the header's switch flips between them; below 960px the menu sheet offers Dark · Light · System. A post follows the system setting until a theme is chosen, and nothing flashes on load. Jost, Newsreader, DM Mono and Michroma are self-hosted through `next/font`; a Pause motion control and reduced-motion support.
- **Sanity CMS**: Code-defined Profile, Blog Post, Project, and structured rich-prose schemas, edited in the Studio embedded at `/studio`.
- **SEO Optimized**: Implements structured data (JSON-LD) and semantic HTML for optimal search engine visibility, with a share image for every page.
- **Plain names**: the header, menu, footer, page titles, breadcrumbs and buttons name the five sections Projects, Writing, Experience, About and Contact. Each section's themed name (Missions, Flight Log, Trajectory, Crew File, Comms) is only a small tag above its page title and on its share card, and no heading is numbered.
- **Home**: the name, the profile's headline and what you are open to over NASA's orbital sunrise, with one action, CV, which opens the CV on `/resume`; then the strongest project on its stage (led by its summary, without stats) and the next two as rows; the latest three posts; and a close: your one-line statement (the tagline, else the introduction's first sentence) with a link to Current focus on About, then one way to `/contact`, your Contact Button to the Hiring topic while there is an Open To line, else Send a message.
- **Writing**: `/blog` is one list, newest first, under the writing introduction and "Follow: RSS · LinkedIn". Each row is the date, reading time, title and standfirst, with a tag once that tag gathers two posts; there is no search, archive or year heading (`/blog/archive` redirects to `/blog`). Tag pages and a full-content RSS feed titled "Adithya Rajendran — Writing". Each entry is a paper-grade long read: its first paragraph on the first screen, a 68-character measure, a contents rail from two sections (a closed box on phones), code listings with a Copy button that break out wide once a line passes 64 characters, photographs and figures with their captions, callouts, footnotes in the margin and in the notes at the end, dated revisions, the previous and next entries, the related project, and a print layout. Only the footnotes are numbered: the crumb is "Writing", with no entry number, and no listing, photograph or figure carries one.
- **Projects**: `/portfolio` shows the featured project on its stage beside its cover (or its model's poster), the next two as tiles and the rest as quieter rows, so the last project is the least prominent; there are no counts, register or stats on the index. Its old section links (`#experience`, `#skills`, `#certifications`, `#engineering-writing`, `#contact`) lead on to where those sections live now. Each project has a page at `/portfolio/<slug>`, under the crumb "Projects". A project with evidence to lay out (a brief that adds to its card, results, lessons or next steps, its 3D model's poster or callouts, or an essay in sections) gets the full file: its status, title and summary, the way to its write-up and its repositories, its stats and facts, then Overview, Results with their notes, Retrospective, Parts of the build, the case study, References and Related writing, each shown only when it is filled in, and the case study only when it says more than the summary, highlights and brief. Any other project is a short note: its title, summary, the highlights that add to it, and its facts and links. Mission numbers order the projects and name them in the Studio, but no page prints one.
- **Experience**: `/resume` is the record in two views under one head (the Experience introduction, what you are open to, and Download CV (PDF) when a PDF is uploaded), switched by Timeline · List. Timeline, the flight through the record in 3D, is the default where motion runs; Future, what you are open to with a quiet Contact, is its rail's last stop. List is the CV (education, experience, projects, writing and talks, skills, certifications): the default under reduced motion and without JavaScript, the view every CV link opens, and always in the HTML. Dates are printed as given, with no derived lengths of time. The page prints as a two-sheet CV whose masthead points to the contact page; `/resume/trajectory` redirects to it.
- **About**: `/about` is a record (your studies, previous role, focus areas, and LinkedIn and GitHub), then the full biography and Current focus, the Right Now list grouped by kind when it holds two kinds or more, each item linking to its post, project or address when it has one. There is no portrait.
- **Automated Testing**: GitHub Actions runs lint, type checks, a check that the Sanity types are current, the formatter, Vitest unit tests, a production build, and Playwright browser tests (smoke, no-JavaScript, axe accessibility in both themes, layout, the orange accent budget, theme and menu behaviour, the header and footer, the contact page, the writing index, a post, the CV, its print and the flight, the projects, the home page, the About page, a byte report, review screenshots and the embedded Studio) against a fixture build. A second workflow runs the same specs against each successful Vercel preview once the `VERCEL_AUTOMATION_BYPASS_SECRET` repository secret exists; until then that job is reported as skipped, not passed.
- **Contact Handling**: `/contact` is the head (the contact introduction and what you are open to), then the message form, whole on the first screen of a laptop. Its optional Topic radios are the routes: internships and roles (shown while the profile says what you are open to), research and collaboration (shown when the profile has a contact invitation, which describes it), consulting (only while it is switched on) and hello. `/contact#hiring` picks a topic on arrival, and the topic prefixes the email subject; a route's prompt is only the message field's placeholder once its topic is chosen. An unsent message survives a reload. The Resend-backed form is protected by Vercel BotID and by rate limiting: the Vercel WAF rule `contact-form` where the plan allows it, else a fallback limit in the code itself. Zod validates every field, and the email's domain must accept mail (an MX check). It needs JavaScript for BotID, so without it the page offers LinkedIn. There is no public email address or phone number. Contact is in the header on every page (in the bar itself on phones), and `/portfolio#contact` leads on to `/contact`.

## Getting Started

### Prerequisites

- Node.js 24.x (`engines` in `package.json`)
- pnpm 10.34.5, pinned by `packageManager` in `package.json` (`corepack enable` picks it up)

### Installation

```bash
pnpm install
```

### Running the Project

**Development:**

```bash
pnpm dev
```

**Production:**

```bash
pnpm build
pnpm start
```

**Tests:**

```bash
pnpm test                               # Vitest unit tests
pnpm exec playwright install chromium   # once, for the browser tests
pnpm test:e2e                           # builds the fixture site and runs Playwright
BASE_URL=https://<preview-url> pnpm test:e2e:preview   # the same specs against a deployment
```

`CLAUDE.md` lists the full pre-merge gate and what each browser spec checks. Reports are written to `playwright-report/`.

## Content and deployment

- Copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` and set the public Sanity project and dataset values. Published content needs no read token. Keep local environment files out of Git.
- Edit your profile, résumé PDF, posts, and projects in `/studio`. Publishing a profile, post or project calls the Sanity webhook, which refreshes every page that shows it, including project pages (the webhook's filter in Sanity must include `project`). The daily scheduled-post refresh (just after midnight UTC) releases future-dated posts the same way, and catches up on any post dated in the last seven days if a run was missed.
- In **Profile → Identity**, edit the current headline, short introduction, and search description. These flow through the homepage, About, search metadata, and social previews. The headline is the line under your name on the home page. The optional **Tagline** is the one-line statement that closes the home page; without one, the introduction's first sentence is used.
- In **Profile → Status**, set **Availability**: the status, **Open To** (one line per opening, printed as written, for example "Summer 2027 internships" and "Full-time opportunities in 2028"; the site joins them with a dot), an optional **Contact Button** (for example "Write about a role") and, for your records, when you last confirmed it. The Open To line is shown under your name on the home page and its share card, in the heads of the CV and of `/contact` (and its share card), and as Future, the Timeline flight's last stop; while it is shown, `/contact` offers the Hiring route, and the Contact Button closes the home page, leading there. Without Open To lines, or with a Closed status, all of them are hidden. **Launch** (a date and event) is stored for reference; the site does not show it. Leave **Open to Consulting** off until the site is on the Vercel Pro plan; switching it on shows the Consulting route. **Talks & Papers** print on the CV after the latest writing.
- In **Profile → Homepage & Writing**, edit focus areas, the Experience introduction, writing introduction and the optional contact invitation, which is the Research route on `/contact`. The featured article and Start Here posts are stored for later use; the home page lists the latest three posts automatically, and unpublished and future-dated posts stay hidden.
- **Profile → Site copy** gathers every page introduction that describes your work: the CV (Experience Introduction), the Blog (writing introduction), **Projects Introduction** (`/portfolio`) and **Contact Introduction** (`/contact`), plus **Contact Routes**: the title and prompt of the Hiring, Research, Consulting and Hello routes (the prompt is the message field's optional placeholder once that topic is chosen). An empty introduction or prompt is simply not shown; a route without a title uses its topic's name.
- In **Profile → About / Right Now**, edit the biography, location, profile links, and the Right Now list. Each Right Now item has a **Kind** (question, building, reading or learning): About lists it as Current focus, dated by **Right Now Updated At** and grouped by kind when it holds two kinds or more; an item with a related post, project or URL links there, and the home page's closing statement links to the list. About shows the whole biography. The portrait field is not shown on the site. Removing a link or optional content hides it on the site.
- In **Profile → Portfolio**, maintain experience, education, skills, credentials, and the résumé PDF. Use **Currently Here** for ongoing work or study and **Expected Graduation Year** for a year-only estimate. When you know only the year a role or degree started or ended, enter any day in that year and set **Start Date Precision** or **End Date Precision** to **Year only**: the site then prints the year alone. Each entry also takes a short organization name for the Timeline flight, a website, an employment type, and an optional change of direction. The optional **Résumé Note** identifies an older PDF while a replacement is being prepared. Profile edits do not rewrite the uploaded PDF.
- Posts own their title, description, body, publication date, and topic tags; Projects own their case studies. Tag pages follow published tags automatically; a tag is linked from the posts once it gathers two of them. In the Studio, posts are under **Flight Log · Posts** (with a **Scheduled** list of posts dated after today, which the daily refresh releases) and projects under **Missions · Projects**, ordered by mission number.
- Each post can also take a **Cover** (with alt text, caption, credit and kind), up to three **Related Projects**, a **Revised At** date for substantive revisions (shown as "Updated" on the post), and a **Changelog** of dated updates and corrections, listed at the end of the post and in the RSS feed. Related projects show at the end of the post, and the cover as its lead photograph after the first paragraph.
- In a post's text, the **Footnote** button (¹) attaches a note to the selected words: the post numbers notes in reading order, sets each beside its line on wide screens and lists them at the end. Images take an optional **Credit**, a **Kind** (photographs are toned to the theme; diagrams, plots and screenshots are shown as drawn; none is numbered, so each caption stands on its own) and a **Width** (text width, wide or the full reading column). Callouts add a **Caution** tone for steps that can break a setup or lose data. Code blocks need nothing new: a listing breaks out wide by itself when a line is longer than 64 characters.
- Each project has a **Title** and an optional **Short Name** ("Homelab"), the words of the previous / next links between project pages; without one, those links use the title. Each has a **Mission Number** (MSN-01 to MSN-99; a new project takes the next free one, and two projects cannot share one), one to three **Types**, and an optional **Featured Slot**: slot 1 is the stage of the home page and `/portfolio`, and slots 2 and 3 come next (the two rows under it on the home page); the other projects follow by mission number, so the last is the least prominent (the Studio warns when two projects share a slot). Status adds **Planned** and **Stopped**, and **Completed** is shown as "Complete". When you know only the years, enter any day in each year and set **Date Precision** to **Year only**; turn on **Dates Are Estimates** to print them as "c. 2023". The optional status note, role, parameters, brief, results, lessons, next steps and **3D Model** (a poster, and callouts listed under Parts of the build) show on the project's page; the portrait cover, the model's description and the callouts' links to an essay or post heading (which the Studio checks) are stored but not shown. A parameter whose value starts with a number ("3", "99.55%", "Zero") is shown as a stat in the project page's head, unless the project has results (then the results table shows the numbers, each with its note) or the page is a short note, which shows no stats. Tiles and the featured stage show no stats. A named value ("Okta OIDC") is listed in the page's facts, or not at all when the stack, the summary or a highlight already names it. A project with no brief that adds to its summary and highlights, and no results, lessons, next steps, model poster, callouts or sectioned essay, is shown as a short note (a cover alone does not change that). A project essay that only restates the summary, highlights and brief is left out, and so is "Read the write-up" when it would lead there.
- Your résumé projects were seeded as Studio drafts by a named migration, reviewed and published before the launch; the website project stays a draft. `migrations/README.md` records each migration and whether it has run on production, including the one still to run, which turns the one legacy link in the homelab post into the link type the Studio writes.
- **Back up and restore.** The content lives only in Sanity, whose history keeps a few days of revisions (three on the free plan). Export the dataset before any migration and after large edits, and keep the tarball off your machine: `pnpm exec sanity dataset export "$NEXT_PUBLIC_STORE_SANITY_DATASET" ~/backups/site-$(date +%F).tar.gz`. `migrations/README.md` has the restore steps.
- The visual theme, navigation, section labels, button wording, artwork, and stable site identity stay in code. Everything that describes you or changes over time comes from the profile, and is left out when its field is empty. No code change or redeploy is needed for the content fields above.
- The mission patch (the AR monogram used as the favicon, the Apple touch icon and the header mark) is drawn by `scripts/generate-patch.mjs`; run `node scripts/generate-patch.mjs` after changing it.
- The home hero is NASA's photograph ISS072-E-30246 (an orbital sunrise, Expedition 72; public domain, credited on the page as "Photo: NASA / Expedition 72" with the frame's ID). The original and its crop boxes, credit and fitted limb geometry live in `assets/artwork/` (`nasa-iss072e030246.jpg`, `hero-sunrise.json`), outside `public/`, so they are not served. `node scripts/generate-hero-sunrise.mjs` writes the 21:9 desktop and 9:16 mobile encodes (AVIF and WebP, with the credit in their XMP metadata) to `public/images/hero-sunrise-v1/` and `lib/hero-sunrise.json`; bump the directory version whenever you re-encode, because `/public` images are cached immutably. Responsive preloads fetch one size in one format, and an inline preview shows until it arrives. In Flight Manual, and in print, the same limb is drawn in ink from the fitted geometry instead.
- The flight's planet maps (from NASA and Solar System Scope) are encoded by `node scripts/encode-trajectory-textures.mjs`; `public/images/trajectory/README.md` lists each map's source and licence. Rename a map when you re-encode it, for the same reason.
- The Vercel project builds GitHub branches as previews and `main` as production. `.env.example` lists every environment variable: the webhook needs `SANITY_REVALIDATE_SECRET`, the daily cron `CRON_SECRET`, and the contact form `RESEND_API_KEY` and `CONTACT_FORM_TO_EMAIL`. Vercel honours the `packageManager` pnpm pin only when `ENABLE_EXPERIMENTAL_COREPACK=1` is set in the project; otherwise it infers pnpm from the lockfile.
- The browser tests also run against every successful Vercel preview (`.github/workflows/e2e-preview.yml`). If previews are protected, create a **Protection Bypass for Automation** secret in Vercel (Settings → Deployment Protection) and store it as the GitHub Actions secret `VERCEL_AUTOMATION_BYPASS_SECRET`; until it exists, that job runs no test and shows as skipped on each PR, not as passed.
- Without the `contact-form` rule (Vercel Firewall → Rate Limit, on Pro or Enterprise), the contact form falls back to the limit in its own code; create the rule if the plan allows it.
- Before merging, run the checks documented in `CLAUDE.md` (the pull request template lists them) and verify a Vercel preview. After a change to the Sanity schema reaches production, run `pnpm exec sanity schema deploy` from an authenticated CLI with the project and dataset variables loaded, so the hosted schema that Sanity's tools read matches the repository. Existing `/blog`, `/portfolio` (its old section fragments included), `/resume`, RSS, and résumé PDF routes are preserved; `/comms` redirects to `/contact`.
